\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We present a protocol for low-shot procedural skill transfer in language-agent
debugging. In a six-task, single-execution PyBugHive \texttt{black} pilot,
trajectory-induced Auto SKILL.md matched the best observed solve rate at 5/6.
Task-level paired analyses favored it over reflection baselines on process
cost, but it tied a human-written checklist on solve rate and cycles, and the
checklist token interval crossed zero. One explicit negative-transfer case
demonstrated the need to measure memory misapplication. A supplementary
\texttt{gpt-5.5} control found that shuffled skill content retained 6/6 solve
rate and exposed outlier-sensitive token means. The study therefore supports
an inspectable evaluation protocol and initial process evidence, while broader
tasks, repeated seeds, multiple annotators, and stronger content controls are
necessary before making general performance or learned-knowledge claims.
